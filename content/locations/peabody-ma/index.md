## Why Does Where You Train Change Which Licensing Rules Apply Later?

Choosing to train in Peabody instead of assuming a certificate transfers anywhere matters because Peabody's own Board of Health regulation names Permanent Make-Up specifically on both permits, and a certificate earned here is not itself the license a graduate will eventually need to work.

In Massachusetts, there is no single state license for body art: General Laws Chapter 111, Section 31 gives each town or city's own Board of Health that authority, which is why the rules are not identical between Adriana's two Massachusetts locations, even under the same ownership. In Peabody specifically, the tattooing establishment permit must specify, in writing, that the location is authorized for Permanent Make-Up application (Section 3.1.5), and each individual Tattoo Artist permit must specify whether that artist is authorized to perform it (Section 3.2.4); without that written authorization, Peabody's own regulation calls the work “strictly prohibited,” even where regular tattooing is allowed.

New Hampshire and Massachusetts also handle transfer differently: a body art license issued by New Hampshire's Office of Professional Licensure and Certification travels statewide, while a Massachusetts town permit stays tied to that one town's Board of Health, so a Wilmington permit does not carry over to Peabody, or the reverse. None of this describes Adriana's Academy's own permit status, which this page does not claim; it describes what a graduate must check herself, since the certificate awarded here is not a license and confirming the requirement is the student's own responsibility, not the school's.
