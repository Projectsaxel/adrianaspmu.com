# About Adriana Souza Santos

Master Permanent Makeup Artist with 18+ years of experience and 5,000+ procedures performed. Trained and practicing in Brazil before founding Adriana’s Permanent Makeup in Massachusetts in 2017. Consultations in English and Portuguese.

New Hampshire Body Artist license 4283, verifiable at the state’s public lookup. Body art licensed by the Wilmington and Peabody Boards of Health and by the Town of Salem, NH. Women-owned business. LGBTQ+ friendly.

## Trained in Brazil, Practicing in New England Since 2017

Adriana began her career in permanent makeup in Brazil, a country with one of the largest and most demanding PMU markets in the world, where the craft is treated as a full profession rather than an add-on service. She spent years there building volume and range across skin tones and brow types that most artists never encounter, and trained in techniques that reached the United States considerably later.

She brought that work to Massachusetts and opened **Adriana’s Permanent Makeup in 2017**. What followed — two studios, an academy in Peabody, more than 5,000 procedures and over 300 students trained — was built on a foundation that already existed. In total she has **more than 18 years** in the industry.

That background is the reason for two things clients notice. The first is **color work on deeper and warmer skin tones**, including dark lip neutralization, which is routine in Brazil and still specialized here. The second is that Adriana and the team **consult in both English and Portuguese**, so a client can describe exactly what she wants in her own language — which matters more than it sounds when the result is on her face for years.

## How Did Adriana’s Grow From One Studio Into Two States?

Adriana’s Permanent Makeup opened as a single studio in Massachusetts in 2017. Since then it has grown into two client-facing studios, Wilmington, MA and Salem, NH, plus a dedicated training academy in Peabody, MA that operates separately from both and serves students only.

The Wilmington studio came first: a private suite at 211 Lowell Street, Suite F, on Massachusetts Route 129. Demand from New Hampshire clients led to the Salem studio, at 117A Main Street on New Hampshire Route 97 in Salem Depot, which has several curtained treatment stations rather than one private room.

Growth also moved into training. Adriana’s Academy, at 39 Cross Street, Suite 206 in Peabody, MA, has certified more than 300 students to date, entirely separate from either client studio.

Massachusetts works differently from New Hampshire. There is no single state body art license in Massachusetts: under General Laws Chapter 111, Section 31, each town’s Board of Health writes and enforces its own rules, which is why the Wilmington licenses carry town numbers rather than state ones.

## What Happens During a Consultation With Adriana?

Every appointment opens with a free consultation before any pigment is placed. Adriana assesses skin type, discusses the client’s goals, and maps a proposed shape or color directly on the client’s skin for approval, in either English or Portuguese depending on the client’s preference.

A client describes the result she wants in her own words rather than through a translated approximation, which matters when the outcome stays on her face for one to three years. A Portuguese-speaking client can specify a lip color or a brow shape precisely, without losing nuance in translation.

Pricing, the healing timeline, and any contraindications are confirmed at this stage too, before a deposit or appointment is booked.

## What Standards Does the Studio Follow for Every Client?

Adriana’s Permanent Makeup uses single-use, sterile needles for every procedure, opened in front of the client at the start of the appointment. Intake and consent forms are completed in person at the studio, not only online, and no procedure is performed on anyone under 18.

These standards apply the same way at both the Wilmington, MA and Salem, NH studios, regardless of which artist performs the service. A client is welcome to ask to see a practitioner’s current license before booking.
