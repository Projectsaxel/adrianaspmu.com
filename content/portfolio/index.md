## How Should I Read a Before-and-After Permanent Makeup Photo?

A before-and-after photo is shaped by four things a viewer rarely gets told: the lighting, the camera angle, whether it was taken fresh or after the skin fully healed, and whether it was retouched. Reading past those four factors is the difference between judging the artist's actual work and judging a well-lit photograph.

Fresh pigment sits close to the surface of the skin and reads darker and more saturated than the same work will look once healing finishes, typically 6 to 8 weeks later — the [healing timeline in the aftercare guide](../aftercare/#healing-timeline) walks through what changes week by week. A photo taken on the day of the procedure is not showing the final color.

Angle and lighting change the same face two different ways. A straight-on photo under even, front-facing light shows symmetry and true color; an angled photo under dramatic side lighting can make strokes look bolder, an arch look higher, or a lip line look crisper than it reads in a mirror at home.

| Factor | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Fresh (day of procedure) vs. healed (6–8 weeks later) | Fresh pigment looks darker and more saturated than the settled, healed result. |
| Lighting | Even, front-facing light vs. dramatic side light | Side lighting exaggerates shadow and can make a stroke or line look bolder than it is. |
| Angle | Straight-on vs. tilted or angled | An angle can hide asymmetry or make an arch look higher than a mirror view would show. |
| Editing | Unretouched vs. filtered | A filter shifts color tone, brightness, and skin texture away from the true result. |
| Consistency | Same client, same lighting, before and after | Mismatched lighting between the two photos can make the "before" look worse than it was. |

The photos in this gallery are not individually labeled as fresh or healed. Anyone comparing a specific photo to their own goals should ask the artist, at consultation, which stage that photo represents before treating it as a guarantee.

## What Does My Own Result Actually Depend On?

A permanent makeup result depends on four factors that vary from client to client: the client's skin type, the natural density of the brow or lip tissue being worked on, how closely aftercare instructions are followed, and how many sessions the plan includes. No photo in this gallery can substitute for a consultation that looks at these four factors on your own skin.

Oilier skin tends to hold powder and shading techniques better than fine hairstroke work, since oil can blur a crisp line over time; drier skin generally holds a fine stroke longer. Adriana Souza Santos assesses skin type at consultation before recommending Nano Brows, Microblading, Powder Brows, or Combination Brows.

Natural brow density changes what a technique can do: strokes drawn over existing hair blend differently than strokes drawn over sparse or absent hair. The same applies to lip tissue — naturally darker lip pigment may need the [dark lip neutralization](../services/lips/dark-lip-neutralization/) step before a [lip blush](../services/lips/lip-blush/) color can show true.

Aftercare is the one factor the client controls directly. The full day-by-day routine, what to avoid, and how healing normally looks are covered in the [aftercare guide](../aftercare/); skipping steps there is one of the most common reasons a healed result differs from the fresh photo taken on procedure day.

Every service already includes one perfecting touch-up, scheduled 6 to 8 weeks after the first visit once healing is complete, at no extra charge. A separate [yearly touch-up](../services/touch-ups/yearly-touch-up/), from $300, refreshes color roughly 12 months later, once natural fading begins.
