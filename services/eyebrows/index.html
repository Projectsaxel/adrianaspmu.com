Which Eyebrow Permanent Makeup Technique Is Right for You?

Adriana’s offers five eyebrow techniques, priced from $550 to $650. They differ in the tool used, the finish they create and how long they last: strokes for a hair-like result, shading for a soft makeup finish, or a combination of both. Your artist recommends one at consultation based on your skin type and brow density.

Nano Brows

Nano Brows uses an ultra-fine needle and digital machine to create realistic, hair-like strokes for fuller, natural-looking eyebrows that last 1 to 3 years.

Microblading

Microblading uses a handheld tool to create fine, hair-like strokes for fuller, natural-looking brows that typically last 12 to 24 months.

Combination Brows

Nano Combo Brows

Strokes or Shading: How Do You Choose?

The real decision is whether your skin and morning routine call for strokes or shading. Oily skin blurs fine strokes as they heal and holds powder shading better, while drier skin keeps a crisp stroke line longer. Combination Brows and Nano Combo suit clients who want both, confirmed at your consultation.

How Does a Brow Appointment Work?

A brow appointment moves through consultation and mapping, numbing, pigment placement, and a final review. A perfecting session follows 6 to 8 weeks later, included in your original price.

Your artist marks a shape before any needle touches your skin, so you approve the design first. That perfecting session is not the same appointment as a Yearly Touch-Up, a separate paid visit roughly 12 months later.

Every eyebrow price includes the consultation, mapping, numbing, the procedure, and the perfecting session 6 to 8 weeks later. It excludes the Yearly Touch-Up, a separate paid visit about 12 months out from $300, priced by how much pigment remains, and every price can be split with Cherry.

Who Should Not Book Eyebrow Permanent Makeup?

Pregnancy, breastfeeding, active isotretinoin use, and being under 18 rule out any eyebrow procedure outright. Diabetes, autoimmune conditions, blood thinners, keloid scarring, and an active skin condition in the brow area need medical clearance first, detailed on the aftercare and contraindications guide.
